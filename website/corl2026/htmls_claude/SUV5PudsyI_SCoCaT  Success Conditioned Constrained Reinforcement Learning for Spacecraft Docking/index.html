SCoCaT: Success Conditioned Constrained Reinforcement Learning for Spacecraft Docking

Aman Arora, Ricard Marsal I Castan, Matteo El-Hariry, Miguel Olivares-Mendez
SnT - Interdisciplinary Centre for Security, Reliability and Trust, University of Luxembourg, Luxembourg
{aman.arora, ricard.marsal, matteo.elhariry, miguel.olivaresmendez}@uni.lu

10th Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2026), Austin TX, USA. Project page: https://snt-spacer.github.io/scocat-cstr-RL-docking/ · Code: https://github.com/snt-spacer/scocat-cstr-RL-docking

Abstract (summary)

Termination-based constrained RL, such as Constraints as Terminations (CaT), has several advantages for safety-critical deployment. It needs no online optimization, scales with one scalar per constraint, and is simpler than Lagrangian methods. The authors identify a failure mode on terminal-navigation tasks, where the goal lies inside a region in which constraints tighten. There, the survival-weighted objective makes "dwelling outside the goal region strictly preferable to entering." They call this "feasibility collapse," formalize it, and resolve it with a minimal PPO augmentation: a dense per-step success signal learned through an auxiliary value critic. The method is validated on a 6U CubeSat (6-DoF) and on an air-bearing floating platform with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

Keywords: Constrained Reinforcement Learning, Spacecraft Docking, Sim-to-Real Transfer

1 Introduction (summary)

Terminal navigation tasks share three properties:

  • a precise goal configuration;
  • safety constraints that concentrate along the approach;
  • failures that are irreversible.

Examples include docking, precision landing, peg-in-hole insertion, surgical tool placement and underwater mating. SWaP-limited platforms need controllers whose runtime cost is only a network forward pass. This rules out MPC and online QP-based shields and motivates deep RL.

CaT stochastically terminates future rewards upon violation, using a survival product \(\prod(1-\delta_t)\). In terminal navigation, however, states just outside the goal corridor offer near-maximal reward at near-zero termination probability, which causes feasibility collapse. SCoCaT adds a per-step success indicator and an auxiliary critic \(V_s\) trained against environment-only terminations, inspired by Roy et al. [15].

Contributions:

  • A formalization of feasibility collapse and of the threshold at which hovering becomes CaT-preferred.
  • SCoCaT, a minimal extension of CaT with a decoupled success signal.
  • Validation on 3-DoF and 6-DoF platforms, including sim-to-real transfer, against PPO, CaT and gradient-projection baselines.

2 Related Work (summary)

Constrained RL. The paper covers three families of approaches:

  • Primal-dual CMDP methods: CPO [11], FOCOPS [13] and PID-Lagrangian [12].
  • Termination-based methods: ET-MDP [14] and CaT [7], which has been extended to loco-manipulation [16].
  • Other approaches: state augmentation (Sauté RL [17]), gradient projection [18, 19], and Direct Behavior Specification [15]. The last is the closest prior work, but it is Lagrangian and off-policy.

RL for spacecraft proximity operations. Prior work includes planar and 6-DoF RL docking [3, 6, 20, 21], run-time assurance and CBF approaches [2, 5, 22, 23], and air-bearing or UAV sim-to-real testbeds [3, 24, 25, 26].

3 Methodology

The setting is an MDP augmented with constraints \(c_i\), subject to the chance constraints

\[\mathbb{P}_{(s,a)\sim\rho^\pi_\gamma}[c_i(s,a)>0]\le\tilde\varepsilon_i\quad\forall i\in\mathcal{I}.\tag{1}\]

3.1 Problem Formulation

Let \(\mathcal{G}\) be the goal region and \(\partial\mathcal{G}^\epsilon=\{s:\mathrm{dist}(s,\mathcal{G})\le\epsilon\}\setminus\mathcal{G}\) the surrounding annulus.

Definition 1 (Feasibility Collapse). A policy concentrates its stationary distribution on \(\partial\mathcal{G}^\epsilon\) while \(\mathbb{P}_\pi(s_t\in\mathcal{G})\approx0\).

Proposition 1. Consider a three-state MDP \(\{s_{far},s_\partial,s_\mathcal{G}\}\) satisfying:

  • (i) \(r(s_\partial)=R>0\) and \(r(s_\mathcal{G})=0\), with \(s_\mathcal{G}\) absorbing;
  • (ii) \(\delta(s_\mathcal{G})=\bar\delta>0\);
  • (iii) \(\delta(s_\partial)=0\).

Then hovering at \(s_\partial\) is a strict local optimum of the CaT objective.

Proposition 1b covers a non-absorbing, rewarding goal. In that case hovering is preferred iff \(R>\kappa r_\mathcal{G}\), where \(\kappa=1\) at \(\bar\delta=0\) and \(\kappa=0.023\) at \(\bar\delta=0.3\).

3.2 Constraints as Terminations

\[J_{\mathrm{CaT}}(\pi)=\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\Big(\prod_{t'=0}^{t-1}\gamma(1-\delta_{t'})\Big)r(s_t,a_t)\Big]\tag{2}\]

Here \(\delta_t=\max_i p_i^{\max}\cdot\mathrm{clip}(c_i^+/c_i^{\max},0,1)\).

3.3 Success-Conditioned Constraints as Terminations (SCoCaT)

The binary success indicator gates on pose and velocity:

\[I_{\mathrm{succ},t}=\mathbf{1}[\|p_t-p_\mathcal{G}\|<\varepsilon_p]\cdot\mathbf{1}[e_{\mathrm{orient},t}<\varepsilon_o]\cdot\mathbf{1}[\|v_t\|<\varepsilon_v]\cdot\mathbf{1}[\|\omega_t\|<\varepsilon_\omega]\tag{3}\]

A continuous variant is also evaluated:

\[I^{\mathrm{cont}}_{\mathrm{succ},t}=e^{-\|p_t-p_\mathcal{G}\|/\sigma_p}e^{-e_{\mathrm{orient},t}/\sigma_o}\big[1-w_t(1-e^{-\|v_t\|/\sigma_v}e^{-\|\omega_t\|/\sigma_\omega})\big]\tag{4}\]

The method uses two advantage streams:

\[A_{r,t}=\mathrm{GAE}(r_t(1-\delta_t),V_r,\delta_t)\tag{5}\] \[A_{s,t}=\mathrm{GAE}(I_{\mathrm{succ},t},V_s,d^{\mathrm{env}}_t)\tag{6}\] \[A^{\mathrm{tot}}_t=\mathrm{standardize}(A_{r,t}+A_{s,t})\tag{7}\]

Proposition 2. Hovering is not a local optimum of the SCoCaT objective, because

\[A^{\mathrm{tot}}(s_\partial\to s_\mathcal{G})=-\frac{R\gamma}{1-\gamma}+\gamma V_s(s_\mathcal{G})\tag{8}\]

is positive once \(V_s(s_\mathcal{G})>R/(1-\gamma)\). The required bound is \(R<1\) for the binary signal, \(R<0.67\) for the continuous signal, and \(R<0.64\) in the finite-horizon worst case. The paper's reward satisfies \(R\le0.099\).

Algorithm 1 SCoCaT-PPO
Require: policy πθ, critics Vr, Vs, constraints {ci, pi_max}
1: for epoch = 1..N do
2:   τ ← CollectTrajectories(πθ)
3:   δt ← max_i pi_max · clip(ci+/ci_max, 0, 1)        [CaT]
4:   τ.rewards ← τ.rewards × (1 − δt)                   [CaT]
5:   τ.dones_cat ← δt                                    [CaT]
6:   Ar ← GAE(τ.rewards, Vr, τ.dones_cat)
7:   As ← GAE(τ.Isucc, Vs, τ.dones_env)                  [SCoCaT]
8:   Atot ← standardize(Ar + As)                         [SCoCaT]
9:   UpdatePolicy(πθ, Atot)
10:  UpdateCritic(Vr, τ.rewards, τ.dones_cat)
11:  UpdateCritic(Vs, τ.Isucc, τ.dones_env)              [SCoCaT]
12: end for

3.4 Implementation Details (summary)

Task. The vehicle must hold the pose within tolerance for 100 consecutive steps.

  • Floating Platform tolerances: 10 mm / 2° during training; 10 mm / 0.1 rad at evaluation.
  • CubeSat tolerances: 10 mm / 10° for both training and evaluation.
  • Velocity gates: \(\|v\|<0.05\) m/s during training, tightened to 0.01 m/s at evaluation; \(\|\omega\|<5°\)/s throughout.
  • Spawn range: 0.01–4.0 m from the goal.

Metrics. Corridor Entry (Ent.), TCR (at least 10% of timesteps in the corridor), GCO, Hold, Declared success, and position and heading error.

Constraint severity tiers.

  • Strict (\(p^{\max}=1.0\)): workspace bounds and collision.
  • Moderate (0.1–0.3): docking velocity, angular rate and fuel.
  • Soft (≤0.1): line of sight and jerk.

4 Experiments (summary)

Platforms. A 3-DoF air-bearing Floating Platform with 8 thrusters, and a 6U CubeSat with 8 oblique binary thrusters.

Setup. Training uses Isaac Lab and skrl PPO with 5 seeds per condition. Baselines are PPO, CaT, CaT with curriculum, and CaT-PEGrad.

4.1 Simulation Results (summary)

Floating Platform.

  • SCoCaT reaches TCR 100% at 98.8% compliance, versus CaT's 0.4% TCR at 99.4%.
  • On declared success, SCoCaT scores 0.906±0.066, while PPO and CaT both score 0.
  • PPO passes through the goal at speed and has 70× the docking-velocity violations.

CubeSat.

  • CaT enters the corridor in only 3.7% of episodes.
  • SCoCaT reaches 0.613 declared success at 0.945 compliance.
  • PPO reaches 0.931 declared success at 0.839 compliance.
  • SCoCaT's seed results are bimodal.

Ablations.

  • Reward density does not gate SCoCaT.
  • The binary signal fails under sparse reward, while the continuous signal recovers.
  • Adding success as a reward bonus breaks constraint enforcement.

5 Conclusion (summary)

SCoCaT resolves feasibility collapse by decoupling the success critic from CaT's survival weighting. It is the only method that meets the declared criterion on the Floating Platform, and it partially breaks collapse on the CubeSat.

6 Limitations (summary)

  • The scalarization uses fixed equal weights.
  • Only a PPO instantiation is evaluated.
  • An angular-velocity sim-to-real gap remains.
  • On the CubeSat, compliance is 2.6 points lower than CaT's (line of sight), and seed results are bimodal.
  • Observation noise is not randomized.
  • The hardware study uses only n = 4 runs per method.
  • Coverage of embodiments and task families is limited.

Appendix A: Proofs (summary)

Proposition 1. Hovering yields \(R/(1-\gamma)\). Entering at step \(k\) yields \(R(1-\gamma^k)/(1-\gamma)\), which is strictly smaller (Eqs. 9–10).

Proposition 1b.

\[R>\kappa r_\mathcal{G},\quad \kappa=\frac{(1-\bar\delta)(1-\gamma)}{(1-\gamma)+\gamma\bar\delta}\tag{11}\]

Proposition 2.

\[J_{\mathrm{SCoCaT}}(\pi')-J_{\mathrm{SCoCaT}}(\pi^\star)=\frac{\gamma^k(1-R)}{1-\gamma}>0\tag{15}\]

With a finite horizon the condition becomes

\[R<\frac{1-\gamma^T}{1-\gamma^{H-k}}\tag{16}\]

Appendix B: Reward Bonus vs. Two-Critic (summary)

Adding \(\lambda_b I_{\mathrm{succ}}\) to the post-CaT reward changes the critic's fixed point to \(V^{aug}=V_r^\delta+\lambda_b\tilde V_s^\delta\), where the success term is damped by CaT terminations. The bonus then competes with the survival penalty. In the dense-reward setting this raises docking-velocity violations 37× and LoS violations 13×. The two-critic design leaves \(A_r\), and therefore the CaT penalty, unchanged.

Appendix C: Training Details (summary)

  • Initialization: randomized attitude; goal placed within 2 m of the origin.
  • Networks: [64, 64] ELU layers for the policy and both critics.
  • Optimization: γ = 0.99, λ = 0.95, learning rate 5e-4 with KL-adaptive scheduling, 4,096 environments, reward scale 0.1.
  • Constraint sensitivity (Table 2): fuel violations fall from 63% to 3% as \(p^{\max}\) rises. LoS stays above 84% at all levels, i.e. it is geometry-limited.
