TransmissiveGS: Residual-Guided Disentangled Gaussian Splatting for Transmissive Scene Reconstruction and Rendering

Zhenyu Liang (HKUST), Xiao Zhang (HKUST), Tianchao Li (HKUST), Jack C.P. Cheng (HKUST), Chi-Keung Tang (HKUST)

Abstract

[Summary] Transmissive scenes entangle near-field reflections on the transmissive surface with transmitted content behind it. This produces dual geometries and dual radiance components. TransmissiveGS addresses this with several components:

  • a dual-Gaussian representation with a deferred shading function;
  • residuals from reconstructing multi-view-consistent content, used as cues to separate reflection from transmission;
  • a reflection light field for near-field reflections;
  • a high-frequency regularization.

The authors also contribute a new synthetic dataset. The method outperforms prior GS-based methods on both synthetic and real scenes.

1 Introduction

[Summary] GS extensions for reflective surfaces exist, but transmissive surfaces remain underexplored. Observed color mixes surface reflection with transmitted radiance. Single-Gaussian-set methods therefore tend to fit the multi-view-consistent transmitted content and poorly reconstruct the glass.

Existing ways of modeling reflections also fall short:

  • Spherical harmonics capture only low-frequency effects.
  • Environment maps assume distant light and suffer parallax errors.
  • EnvGS lacks supervision when reflections are weak.
  • Ref-GS has limited high-frequency capacity.

TransmissiveGS introduces reflection and scattering Gaussians, a deferred shading composite, residual-guided disentangling, and a reflection light field.

Contributions:

  • a dual-Gaussian representation with deferred shading;
  • a residual-guided reconstruction strategy;
  • a reflection light field conditioned on surface position and reflection direction;
  • a new synthetic benchmark dataset.

2 Related Work

Inverse Rendering. [Summary] This line of work spans several families:

  • NeRF-based methods (NeRFactor, PhySG, NeRO, NeILF++, Ref-NeRF);
  • GS-based forward shading, deferred shading, near-field ray tracing, and implicit MLP approaches;
  • refraction methods for fully transparent objects;
  • TR-Gaussians and RT-GS, which are limited to planar or semi-transparent surfaces;
  • TSGS, which handles transparent objects without reflections.

Disentanglement of Reflection and Transmission. [Summary] Prior work includes 2D reflection-removal methods, NeRFReN, MS-NeRF, NeuS-HSR, RA-NeRF, Flash-Splat, DC-Gaussian, GlassGaussian and TR-Gaussians. These methods either assume planar surfaces or do not reconstruct the transmissive geometry.

3 Preliminary

[Summary] The method builds on 2DGS. Each disk is parameterized by its center \(\mathbf{p}_k\), tangents \(\mathbf{t}_u,\mathbf{t}_v\), scales \(s_u,s_v\), opacity \(\alpha\), and SH coefficients \(\mathbf{c}\).

\[ P(u,v)=\mathbf{p}_k+s_u\mathbf{t}_u u+s_v\mathbf{t}_v v=H(u,v,1,1)^\top \tag{1} \] \[ \mathcal{G}(\mathbf{u})=\exp\left(-\frac{u^2+v^2}{2}\right) \tag{2} \] \[ \mathbf{x}=(xz,yz,z,1)^\top=WH(u,v,1,1)^\top \tag{3} \] \[ \mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{c}_i\alpha_i\hat{\mathcal{G}}_i(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}))\prod_{j=1}^{i-1}\left(1-\alpha_j\hat{\mathcal{G}}_j(\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}))\right) \tag{4} \]

4 TransmissiveGS

4.1 Dual-GS Representation and Deferred Shading Function

[Summary] The scene uses two Gaussian sets:

  • Reflection Gaussians \(\mathcal{G}_\text{refl}\) model the reflective and transmissive surfaces. They add a Fresnel vector \(\mathbf{f}_0\in\mathbb{R}^3\) and a reflectivity \(r\).
  • Scattering Gaussians \(\mathcal{G}_\text{scat}\) model the rest of the scene.

The two sets are composited as follows:

\[ I=(1-R)I_\text{scat}+RI_\text{refl} \tag{5} \] \[ I_\text{refl}=F\,\Phi_\text{refl}(\mathbf{x}^\text{refl},\omega_r) \tag{6} \] \[ \omega_r=\omega_o-2(\omega_o^\top\mathbf{n}_\text{refl})\mathbf{n}_\text{refl} \tag{7} \] \[ F=F_0+(1-F_0)\left(1-\mathbf{n}_\text{refl}^\top(-\omega_o)\right)^5 \tag{8} \]

4.2 Residual-Guided Disentangling Reconstruction

[Summary] The reconstruction proceeds in two steps:

  1. Stage I fits only \(\mathcal{G}_\text{scat}\). Reflective regions are left unexplained, which produces the residual below.
  2. Stage II initializes \(\mathcal{G}_\text{refl}\) from \(\mathcal{G}_\text{scat}\), freezes \(\mathcal{G}_\text{scat}\), and colors each reflection Gaussian through a learnable environment map.
\[ \mathbf{r}=I_\text{GT}-I_\text{scat} \tag{9} \] \[ \mathbf{c}_i^\text{refl}=\mathbf{f}_{0,i}\cdot E(\omega_{r,i}) \tag{10} \] \[ R(I_\text{refl}-I_\text{scat})=\mathbf{r} \tag{11} \]

[Summary] Because only correct normals retrieve the correct radiance from \(E\), the residuals act as implicit geometric supervision.

4.3 Reflection Light Field

[Summary] The reflection light field is an MLP with 8 layers of width 256, ReLU activations, and a sigmoid output.

  • Inputs: Fourier-encoded position and reflection direction, with \(L=12\). Positions are normalized to \([-1,1]\).
  • Staged fusion: the first four layers process position only. Direction is concatenated at layer five, where position is also re-injected via a skip connection.
  • Training: reflection Gaussian geometry is frozen, while the scattering Gaussians remain learnable.
\[ \gamma(p)=\left(\sin(2^0\pi p),\cos(2^0\pi p),\ldots,\sin(2^{L-1}\pi p),\cos(2^{L-1}\pi p)\right) \tag{12} \]

4.4 Training

\[ \mathcal{L}_c=(1-\lambda)\|I-I_\text{GT}\|_1+\lambda\mathcal{L}_\text{D-SSIM}(I,I_\text{GT}) \tag{13} \]

Here \(\lambda=0.2\).

\[ \mathcal{L}_n^{(\cdot)}=\sum\left(1-\mathbf{n}_{(\cdot)}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{(\cdot)}\right) \tag{14} \]

[Summary] The high-frequency loss averages the error over pixels whose error is above the \(p\)-th percentile \(\tau_p\).

\[ \mathcal{L}_{hf}=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}\mathbb{I}(e(\mathbf{x})>\tau_p)e(\mathbf{x})\Big/\left(\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}\mathbb{I}(e(\mathbf{x})>\tau_p)+\epsilon\right) \tag{15} \] \[ \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_c+\lambda_n^\text{refl}\mathcal{L}_n^\text{refl}+\lambda_n^\text{scat}\mathcal{L}_n^\text{scat}+\lambda_{hf}\mathcal{L}_{hf} \tag{16} \]

5.2 Baseline Comparisons

[Summary] The baselines are 3DGS, 2DGS, GaussianShader, Ref-Gaussian, Ref-GS and EnvGS, each run with its official code and configurations.

  • Rendering: TransmissiveGS performs best overall, capturing sharp near-field reflections while preserving the transmitted interiors.
  • Geometry: EnvGS is excluded from the MAE comparison because it uses a StableNormal prior. Even with that prior, it fails on computer, dome and facade.

5.3 Ablation Studies

[Summary] Ablations are run on the facade scene:

  • w/o Dual-Gaussian: using reflection Gaussians alone fails to reconstruct the glass geometry.
  • w/o Reflection MLP: replacing the light field with an environment map blurs reflections and introduces parallax errors.
  • w/o Decoupling: without the stage-wise freezing, the two Gaussian sets compete and degrade results.
  • w/o High-frequency Loss: fine reflection details become blurred.

6 Conclusion

[Summary] TransmissiveGS combines a dual-Gaussian representation, residual-guided disentanglement and a reflection light field. It outperforms existing methods in both inverse rendering and transmissive surface reconstruction. Its main limitation is the assumption of static content; dynamic scenes are left for future work.

B Additional Ablation Studies on Reflection Light Field.

[Summary] Architecture ablations on the garage scene show the following:

  • Removing staged fusion hurts most.
  • The skip connection helps.
  • Reducing depth or width degrades results.
  • \(L\) must be large enough, but overly large values add noise.
