Déjà View: Looping Transformers for Multi-View 3D Reconstruction

Alessandro Burzio*1,2, Tobias Fischer*1,4, Sven Elflein1,3, Qunjie Zhou1, Riccardo de Lutio1, Jiawei Ren1, Jiahui Huang1, Shengyu Huang1, Marc Pollefeys4, Laura Leal-Taixé1, Zan Gojcic†1, Haithem Turki†1

1NVIDIA 2University of Modena and Reggio Emilia, AImageLab 3University of Toronto, Vector Institute 4ETH Zürich
*Equal contribution †Equal supervision

Abstract

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction transformers have grown past a billion parameters. Citing evidence that contiguous transformer layers act like repeated similar operations [Jacobs et al., 2026] and that multi-view reconstruction transformers refine predictions progressively across depth [Starý et al., 2025], the authors argue that depth partly "buys iteration" inefficiently, and make iteration explicit. DéjàView applies a single looped transformer block recurrently to per-view features for \(K\) steps; trained once, \(K\) becomes an inference-time compute knob. It matches or outperforms much larger baselines on five benchmarks (indoor, outdoor, object-centric, driving) with a fraction of the parameters and comparable or lower compute. The shared-weight loop also outperforms an otherwise identical variant with independent per-step parameters under matched data and compute, suggesting explicit iteration is a stronger inductive bias. Code and model: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/dvl/projects/dvlt/.

1 Introduction

Classical SfM pipelines are inherently iterative (matching, pose estimation, triangulation, bundle adjustment). Recent feed-forward methods replaced this with end-to-end transformers, with gains driven largely by scaling capacity. The authors argue iteration has been absorbed into depth: ViT layers can be replaced by few recurrent applications of a looped block [Jacobs et al., 2026], and DUSt3R's decoder refines pointmaps layer-by-layer [Starý et al., 2025].

DéjàView makes this explicit: starting from DINOv2 features, a single looped block is applied \(K\) times, with \(K\) sampled from \([K_{\min}, K_{\max}]\) in training. The trained recurrence does not converge to a fixed point; instead the state's direction aligns progressively with its endpoint ("directional refinement").

Contributions:

  • DéjàView, a looping transformer applying one shared block recurrently to a DINOv2-initialized state, conditioned on a continuous time interval.
  • A variable-\(K\) training recipe yielding a single checkpoint with compute as an inference-time knob.
  • State-of-the-art quality across five benchmarks at a fraction of parameters and comparable or lower compute.

2 Related Work

Multi-view 3D reconstruction. From classical SfM through MVSNet, DUSt3R/MASt3R, and VGGT-style joint multi-view transformers (Pi3, DA3, MapAnything, VGGT-Ω) and their extensions, all use fixed-depth architectures with many unique parameters; DéjàView frames reconstruction as iterative refinement.

Iterative refinement. RAFT-style GRU updaters (stereo, SLAM, MVS) iterate on top of a fixed backbone; iLRM treats unshared layers as optimization steps over a decoupled scene representation. DéjàView instead interleaves cross-view reasoning and refinement within one shared full transformer block operating on per-view tokens.

Weight-tied transformers. Universal Transformers, ALBERT, and follow-ups in language and algorithmic tasks; RAPTOR shows ViTs can be distilled into looped blocks. DéjàView adopts RAPTOR's gated block but trains end-to-end on a 3D loss without distillation, surpassing a decoupled variant (Table 4).

3 Method

3.1 Problem setup

Given \(V\) images \(\{\mathbf{I}_i\}_{i=1}^V\), \(\mathbf{I}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 3}\), the model predicts per-view depth \(\mathbf{D}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W}\) and ray maps \(\mathbf{R}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{H\times W\times 6}\) (origin \(\mathbf{R}^o\), direction \(\mathbf{R}^d\)) in the first view's frame, with \(\mathbf{X} = \mathbf{R}^o + \mathbf{D}(u,v)\cdot\mathbf{R}^d\). Rotations, translations and intrinsics are recovered from rays following Lin et al. [2025].

3.2 Hypothesis

The authors hypothesize the implicit refinement seen in prior analyses can be made explicit via a looped block performing directional refinement. Unlike RAFT, only the final step is supervised, sparing \((K-1)\) decoder passes. The recurrence is a time-conditioned update over \(0=t_0<\dots \[\mathbf{z}_{k+1} = f_\theta(\mathbf{z}_k,\, t_k,\, t_{k+1}) \tag{1}\]

Continuous-time conditioning decouples the block from any specific \(K\).

3.3 Architecture

Patch encoder and tokens. \(\mathbf{z}_0\) comes from DINOv2 patch tokens (\(\frac{H}{P}\times\frac{W}{P}\)), plus \(R\) register tokens and a camera token (separate parameters for the reference view), 2D RoPE for patches, and a sentinel position for special tokens.

Decoder heads. Two parallel branches (shallow transformer + head): a ray decoder with linear pixel-shuffle head, and a depth decoder with a convolutional head (after Wang et al. [2025a]) producing depth and confidence \(c_\mathbf{D}\). A camera MLP head decodes \(\mathbf{c}_\theta=(\mathbf{t}_\theta,\mathbf{q}_\theta,\mathbf{f}_\theta)\) from camera tokens as a faster alternative; ray-derived recovery is the default. \(\mathbf{X}_\theta = \mathbf{R}^o_\theta + \mathbf{D}_\theta\cdot\mathbf{R}^d_\theta\).

3.4 Looped Block

Block design. Frame attention then global attention (VGGT-style), each pre-norm Attn+MLP with LayerScale, gated by time-conditioned scales:

\[\begin{aligned} \mathbf{z}' &= \mathbf{z}_k + \mathbf{s}_\text{attn}\odot \text{LS}_1(\text{Attn}(\text{LN}_1(\mathbf{z}_k))),\\ \mathbf{z}'' &= \mathbf{z}' + \mathbf{s}_\text{mlp}\odot \text{LS}_2(\text{MLP}(\text{LN}_2(\mathbf{z}'))),\\ \mathbf{z}_{k+1} &= \mathbf{s}_\text{out}\odot \mathbf{z}'' \end{aligned}\tag{2}\]

with \(\mathbf{s} = 1 + \text{MLP}(\gamma(t_k,t_{k+1}))\) from a zero-initialized MLP over sinusoidal embeddings.

Variable step count. \(K\sim\text{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)\), scaled and rounded into \([K_{\min},K_{\max}]\), with \(t_k=k/K\). At inference, \(K_\text{inf}\) trades compute for accuracy within the trained range; it degrades far outside it.

Directional refinement. Unlike DEQs, there is no fixed point: \(\|\mathbf{z}_k\|\) grows, but \(\cos(\mathbf{z}_k,\mathbf{z}_K)\to 1\) and the relative update decays from \(\sim 0.5\) to \(\sim 0.1\). The decoder's pre-norm absorbs the parallel component, so the decoded representation converges in direction.

3.5 Training

Losses. Predictions and ground truth are normalized separately with

\[s = \left(\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{j\in\Omega}\lVert \mathbf{X}_j\rVert_2\right)^{-1}, \tag{3}\] \[\begin{split}\mathcal{L} ={}& \lVert \hat{s}\,\mathbf{D}_\theta - \bar{s}\,\mathbf{D}\rVert_2 + \mathcal{L}_\text{grad}(\hat{s}\,\mathbf{D}_\theta,\bar{s}\,\mathbf{D}) + \lVert\hat{s}\,\mathbf{R}_\theta - \bar{s}\,\mathbf{R}\rVert_1\\ &+ \lVert \hat{s}\,\mathbf{X}_\theta - \bar{s}\,\mathbf{X}\rVert_2 + \mathcal{L}_\text{cam}(\mathbf{c}_\theta,\mathbf{c}),\end{split}\tag{4}\]

where \(\mathcal{L}_\text{grad}\) is a multi-scale \(\ell_1\) depth-gradient loss and \(\mathcal{L}_\text{cam}\) is weighted \(\ell_1\) on translation, rotation and FoV.

Optimization. Stage 1 trains end-to-end with plain \(\ell_2\) and a linear depth head. Stage 2 swaps in the convolutional depth head and finetunes only the depth decoder with confidence-weighted loss \(\mathcal{L}_\text{unc}(c,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}) = c\lVert\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\rVert_2 - \lambda_c\log c\); ray and camera losses are disabled.

4 Experiments

Implementation. PyTorch, 128 H100s, \(V\in[2,18]\) views at 504 px, up to 4,608 images and about 2.5M tokens per step. DINOv2 ViT-B (dim 768, \(P=14\), \(R=4\)). The decoders use dim 384 and 2 blocks each. \(K\sim\text{Beta}(2,1)\) on \([8,16]\), with \(K_\text{inf}=16\). AdamW at lr \(3\times10^{-4}\), weight decay 0.05, cosine schedule, and a 0.1× backbone multiplier, for 200K iterations. Stage 2 runs 40K iterations at \(1\times10^{-4}\) with 500-step warmup and \(\lambda_c=0.2\). Losses are weighted equally; camera terms are weighted (1, 1, 0.5). The gradient loss is applied only on synthetic data. Training uses 29 public datasets.

5 Discussion

DéjàView shows that a single shared recurrent block with variable step count can match or surpass much larger feed-forward reconstructors, suggesting that parameter scaling is not the only path forward. Limitations: (1) no extrapolation beyond \(K_{\max}\), where some channels diverge, and looped-transformer stabilization attempts plateaued at extra cost; (2) variable-\(K\) matches rather than exceeds fixed-\(K\); (3) no explicit handling of dynamic scenes.

C Emergent Iterative Correspondence Search

For a query patch in view 0, the head-averaged global attention is visualized at each iteration:

\[\bar a^{(t)} = \tfrac{1}{H}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(q^{(t)}_h K^{(t)\top}_h/\sqrt{d_h}\right).\]

Attention starts diffuse and concentrates on corresponding patches, resolving symmetries, even though there is no matching supervision.

D Scaling Beyond \(K_{\max}\)

Sweeping \(K_\text{inf}\) beyond 16 shows that AUC@3° peaks near the trained budget and then degrades. AUC@30° and Rel. L2 stay stable to about \(K_\text{inf}=30\) before collapsing. The cause is unbounded growth in a few feature channels, extrapolating the norm drift already visible in Figure 3b.

E Scaling Below \(K_{\max}\)

A full \(K_\text{inf}\)-step pass with matching time conditioning strictly dominates early-stopping a \(K_{\max}\) rollout. At \(K_\text{inf}=8\), Pose AUC@3° rises from 0.31 to 0.44, about a 43% relative improvement.

G Societal Impact

The model lowers the cost of high-quality 3D reconstruction. Because it outputs geometry rather than imagery, the risk of deceptive media is lower, though pipelines that combine it with generative rendering should consider provenance signals such as watermarking. Training used 128 H100s, comparable to peers. Inference uses roughly an order of magnitude fewer parameters and under 5 GiB at 24 views.
