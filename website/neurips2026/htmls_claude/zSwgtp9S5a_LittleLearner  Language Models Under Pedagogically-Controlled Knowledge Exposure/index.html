LittleLearner: Language Models Under Pedagogically Controlled Knowledge Exposure

Fanfei Li* (MPI-IS, Ellis Institute), Jana Zeller* (MPI-IS, Ellis Institute, ETHZ), Manuel Prada-Corral (MPI-IS, Ellis Institute, ETHZ), Thaddäus Wiedemer (MPI-IS, Ellis Institute), Prasanna Mayilvahanan (MPI-IS, Ellis Institute), Ryan Cotterell (ETHZ), Wieland Brendel (MPI-IS, Ellis Institute)

*Equal contribution. Correspondence to fanfei.li@tuebingen.mpg.de. 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026).

Abstract

[Summary] Because web-scale pretraining corpora are heterogeneous, it is hard to know what a model has already seen. The authors introduce LittleCurriculum, an 88B-token corpus restricted to U.S. elementary school (K–5) material, and train LittleLearner, a 5B-parameter model, on it from scratch. The model is linguistically competent but has interpretable knowledge boundaries tied to curriculum standards. Initial experiments show that post-training and in-context learning help the model use existing knowledge but do not raise out-of-scope capabilities.

1 Introduction

[Summary] Model behavior is shaped by pretraining exposure (contamination, reversal curse, data patches), so it is unclear whether ICL or post-training creates new capabilities or merely elicits latent ones. Rather than designing ever-harder benchmarks, the authors constrain the training distribution itself using a developmental (curriculum-based) boundary, complementing temporal cut-offs studied concurrently.

2 Related Work

[Summary] Covers post-hoc knowledge-boundary probing of trained LLMs; evidence that pretraining data dominates downstream behavior and that RL often elicits existing patterns; out-of-distribution benchmarks (HLE, GPQA, MMLU-Pro, MATH-B); and constrained pretraining (BabyLM restricts quantity; a concurrent pre-1931 model restricts time). LittleCurriculum instead restricts conceptual scope.

3 Constructing the LittleLearner–LittleCurriculum Sandbox

3.1 Constructing a Developmentally Restricted LittleCurriculum

3.1.1 Age-of-Acquisition Pre-filtering

[Summary] AoA gives the best grade separation among rule-based proxies; missing AoA values (10% of words) are imputed from Zipf word frequency. Documents with more than 5% of words above AoA 12 are discarded.

3.1.2 LLMJ Annotation and Classifier Training

[Summary] An LLM judge, with CCSS-based prompts optimized via DSPy and OpenEvolve, labels a subset; full annotation would cost about USD 46M.

3.1.3 Curriculum-Based Classification

[Summary] A FastText classifier, then a 50× costlier ModernBERT classifier on ~266M samples agreeing with AoA.

3.1.4 Symbolic Filtering

[Summary] Regex filter for algebraic/calculus notation; removes ~0.1% additional documents.

3.1.5 Frequency Sampling

[Summary] Terms disproportionately associated with Beyond-K–5 material are used to exclude documents during final sampling.

3.1.6 Validation

[Summary] On CommonCoreText, Beyond-K–5 retention is 0% with ~35% K–5 retained; removed K–5 passages are longer and lexically more complex. On WeeBit, 2.48% of 6,000 Beyond-K–5 passages are retained, with only three (0.05%) containing genuinely out-of-scope concepts. A scan for 126 Beyond-K–5 n-grams matches 0.09% of retained passages.

3.2 Training LittleLearner

[Summary] 5B Qwen3-style model trained from scratch for 100 hours on 8 NVIDIA B200 GPUs.

3.3 Validating LittleLearner's Knowledge Exposure

[Summary] Compared against Unfiltered (same recipe on unfiltered FineWeb-Edu) and Gemma 2B (2T tokens).

3.3.1 Language Familiarity Validation

[Summary] BPB rises with linguistic difficulty only for LittleLearner. On CoMTA math dialogues, it matches baselines on elementary content but degrades on algebra, trigonometry, and calculus.

3.3.2 Capability Validation

[Summary] Factual knowledge drops sharply beyond K–5. On MathCAMPS the gap widens gradually with grade; even at pass@1024 on Grade 8, LittleLearner solves fewer than half as many problems as Unfiltered. Skill acquisition does not follow human curriculum order (e.g., multi-digit divisors easier than single-digit).

4 Probing Capabilities with LittleLearner

4.1 Can Model Size Improve Out-of-Scope Performance?

[Summary] Scaling improves K–5 and Grades 6–7 partially; Grade 8 stays at floor. At 0.6B, LittleLearner beats Unfiltered in K–5.

4.2 Can Post-Training through GRPO Recover Out-of-Scope Capabilities?

[Summary] SFT then GRPO; Beyond-K–5 gains are modest for LittleLearner and larger for Unfiltered; K–5 vs. unfiltered GRPO data makes no difference for LittleLearner.

4.3 Can In-Context Learning Recover Performance on Out-of-Scope Tasks?

4.4 What Determines LittleLearner's Capability Ceiling?

[Summary] Across scaling, post-training and ICL, the pretraining filter—not the intervention—sets the ceiling, consistent with prior work; the authors frame this as a starting point.

5 Exploration Avenues: Beyond the K–5 Boundary

[Summary] Proposed directions: RL-driven discovery and algorithmic extrapolation; continual learning, interpretability and calibration; educational science and human–model comparison (without treating the model as a child); interactive and memory-based learning.

6 Conclusion

[Summary] The corpus and model provide a tractable controlled sandbox; injection via post-training or ICL failed to unlock out-of-scope abilities; the approach extends to other constraints and scales, though 5B may understate emergent behaviors.

A.2 LLMJ

[Summary] Four grade bands; majority vote across seed, OpenEvolve and DSPy/GEPA prompts on Gemini 3 Flash, ties broken toward higher grade. ICL and confidence weighting gave no gains. Cost: $0.0066/sample, about $45.87M for 6.95B samples. Single-judge Gemini labeling beat cross-model agreement; a safety adjustment hurt accuracy and recall.

A.4 Frequency Sampling

[Summary] The ~100B-token retained pool exceeds the 80B target; terms are scored by

\[ \mathrm{score}(w) = \log_2 \frac{\mathrm{rate}_{\text{Beyond-K–5}}(w)}{\mathrm{rate}_{\text{K–5}}(w)}, \qquad \mathrm{rate}(w) = \frac{\mathrm{count}(w)}{\text{total tokens}} \]

and high-scoring frequent terms form a blocklist used to downsample documents.

[Summary] WeeBit (Grades 2–4: 2,218; Grades 6–10: 6,000 passages): 2.48% Beyond-K–5 retained, mostly artifacts or simple prose; three out-of-scope passages (0.05%). N-gram audit: 0.09% of retained passages match.

[Summary] Examples dropped include neuroscience and cardiology texts (AoA), homebrewing and a counterfeit-coin algorithm page (FastText), medical articles (ModernBERT), physics homework with exponent notation (Symbolic), and miscellaneous web pages (Frequency sampling).

B Details on Model Training

[Summary] Qwen3-dense architecture, Megatron-Core 0.17.1, Muon optimizer, BF16/MXFP8. Cooloff mix: 91% K–5 pretraining, 5% K–5-rewritten math reasoning, 2% K–5-rewritten math instruction, 2% general instruction data. Custom tokenizer trained on K–5 data only. A 2B single-digit tokenization ablation raised Beyond-K–5 pass@1 (LittleLearner 1.1%→1.8%, Unfiltered 4.6%→10.3%) without closing the gap. The checkpoint with the lowest validation BPB is used.

C.2.2 Performance Across Learning Paths

[Summary] The models diverge mainly at Grade 7, especially on pure-arithmetic decimal framing. Progress along prerequisite chains is non-monotonic, so the model should not be read as a "digital 12-year-old."

C.2.3 Pass@k Performance on MathCAMPS

[Summary] At pass@1024, LittleLearner retains most of Unfiltered's competence through Grade 6 but only about two-thirds at Grade 8. Per-problem success correlates with Unfiltered within K–5 (Spearman 0.80). Curves plateau by k≈100, indicating a capability ceiling rather than a sampling artifact.

C.4 Details on Post-Training Experiments

[Summary] SFT on 108k math CoT and 526k reading-comprehension examples (~46M tokens, one epoch). GRPO on synthetic MathCAMPS-style problems plus GSM8K (filtered for K–5), in 150–300-step segments with re-banding to problems solved 1–15 of 16 times.

[Summary] Three questions per standard generated with Gemini 3 Flash, with grade-appropriate explanations generated using a constrained prompt (concrete numbers, no variables, K–5 vocabulary). Hand-authored natural-prose CoTs serve as shots for 43 K–5 standards. Compared styles: compact algebraic, natural prose, and Q/A only.
