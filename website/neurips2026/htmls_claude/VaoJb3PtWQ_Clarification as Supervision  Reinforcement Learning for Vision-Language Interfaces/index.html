Clarification as Supervision: Reinforcement Learning for Vision-Language Interfaces

John Gkountouras, ILLC, University of Amsterdam (i.gkountouras@uva.nl)
Ivan Titov, ILLC, University of Amsterdam; ILCC, University of Edinburgh (ititov@inf.ed.ac.uk)

Abstract

[Summary] Text-only reasoners are strong at math, but visual reasoning through a captioning interface fails when captions written for humans omit details reasoners need. The authors propose Adaptive-Clarification Reinforcement Learning (AC-RL). During training, the reasoner may request clarification, and success that requires clarification is penalized. This pushes the captioner toward comprehensive initial captions. AC-RL improves average accuracy by 4.4 points over pretrained baselines across seven visual math benchmarks. Analysis shows it would cut clarification requests by up to 39% if they were allowed. The paper frames clarification as implicit supervision that requires no explicit annotations.

1 Introduction

[Summary] Decoupled architectures (e.g., COLA, ViCor, VCTP, VisProg) route visual information through a linguistic bottleneck. This lets existing text reasoners be reused, but it creates an alignment problem: captioners must learn what a specific reasoner needs. Annotating "ideal captions" per reasoner is infeasible.

[Summary] Binary task rewards are sparse, language action spaces are large, and the feedback is indirect. AC-RL addresses this with a tiered reward: full reward for direct success, partial reward \(\alpha = 0.7\) for success after clarification, and zero for failure. This densifies the signal, while the penalty \(1-\alpha\) pushes toward self-sufficient captions.

Contributions:

  • An exploration-based framework for discovering, via RL, what visual information a reasoner requires.
  • A clarification-aware reward structure that uses interaction patterns as learning signals.
  • Empirical gains on seven mathematical VQA benchmarks, plus reduced clarification dependency.

2 Related Work

Reinforcement learning for reasoning in language models. [Summary] DeepSeek-R1 and visual extensions (Skywork-R1V2, Vision-R1, VL-Rethinker, OpenVLThinker, R1-OneVision, Mulberry) extend reasoning chains. AC-RL instead shapes the perception–reasoning interface.

Decoupled perception–reasoning and interface design. [Summary] COLA, VCTP and VisProg use fixed protocols. AC-RL learns caption features from interaction.

Learning alignment through interaction. [Summary] RACRO aligns a captioner using binary rewards. AC-RL densifies this signal. Related approaches include LAMOC, VLRM, OmniCaptioner, Critic-V, and multi-agent frameworks such as AutoGen.

3 Methodology

3.1 The Vision-Reasoner Interface Problem

[Summary] A trainable captioner \(\mathcal{V}_\theta\) maps an image \(I\) and question \(Q\) to text. A frozen reasoner \(\mathcal{R}\) produces the answer \(A\). Clarification requests serve as implicit supervision.

3.2 Training and Inference Protocols

[Summary] Training proceeds as follows. The captioner produces \(c_0 \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot \mid I, Q)\). The reasoner either answers or asks a question \(q_1\). If it asks, a frozen model replies with \(c_1 \sim \pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot \mid I, Q, q_1)\). Because the clarifier is frozen, the captioner must learn to front-load information. Inference is single-pass and uses only \(c_0\).

3.3 Clarification-Aware Reward Design

\[R(\tau)=\begin{cases}1 & \text{if correct answer without clarification}\\ \alpha & \text{if correct answer with clarification}\\ 0 & \text{if incorrect answer}\end{cases}\tag{1}\]

[Summary] Here \(\alpha \in (0,1)\), and the paper sets \(\alpha = 0.7\). The partial reward gives credit when a caption is nearly sufficient. The penalty favors self-sufficient captions.

3.4 Policy Optimization

\[J(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{(I,Q)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi_\theta}[R(\tau)]\right]-\beta\cdot D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi_\theta\|\pi_{\text{ref}})\tag{2}\]

[Summary] Advantages are estimated with BNPO. Gradients flow only through \(c_0\).

4 Experiments

[Summary] The experiments test three hypotheses:

  1. AC-RL beats pretrained and binary-reward baselines.
  2. The clarification scaffold matters.
  3. The gains come from front-loading information into the initial caption.

System architecture. The captioners are InternVL3-2B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B. The reasoner is DeepSeek-R1-Qwen-32B.

Configurations. Standalone VLM; Pretrained + Reasoner; Binary-Reward RL (RACRO-like); AC-RL. All RL methods are trained on ViRL-39K.

Evaluation. All results use single-pass evaluation. Clarification-enabled runs are used only for the behavioral analyses. The benchmarks are MathVista (testmini), MathVision, MathVerse, MMMU (val), WeMath (strict), DynaMath (worst-case) and LogicVista, evaluated with EvalScope and VLMEvalKit.

[Summary] With Qwen-3B, AC-RL raises the average from 39.0 to 43.4 (+4.4), with large gains on DynaMath (+10.6) and MathVerse (+5.2). InternVL-2B gains +3.3. There are minor regressions on WeMath.

4.4 Subject-Level Performance Analysis

[Summary] The largest gains are on quantitative and structural subjects. On MathVision: angles +10.4, transformation geometry +8.3, algebra +7.5. On DynaMath: solid geometry +18.7, algebra +15.1, puzzle tests +13.5. Gains across DynaMath education levels are +6.5, +6.3 and +4.3.

5 Conclusion

[Summary] Interface alignment between modules can be learned through interaction, using clarification as implicit feedback. Future work includes multi-turn clarification with decaying rewards, bidirectional co-adaptation of both modules, and other language-producing tools.

A AC-RL Algorithm

A.1 Formal Problem Setup

[Summary] The MDP is \(\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},P,R,\gamma)\), with \(\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{I}\times\mathcal{Q}\times\mathcal{H}\), \(\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{C}\), \(R:\mathcal{T}\to[0,1]\) and \(\gamma=1\). An episode runs as follows:

  • The policy emits \(c_0\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s_0)\).
  • The reasoner samples \(q_1\sim p(\cdot\mid s_0,c_0)\).
  • If a question is asked, the frozen model replies with \(c_1\sim\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot\mid s_1)\), where \(s_1=(I,Q,\{c_0,q_1\})\).
  • The reasoner then answers.

All post-action mechanisms are \(\theta\)-independent.

A.2 Policy Update

\[\mathcal{L}_{\text{clip}}(\theta)=-\mathbb{E}_{(s_t,a_t)}\left[\min\left(r_t(\theta)A_t,\operatorname{clip}(r_t(\theta),1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)A_t\right)\right]+\beta D_{KL}(\pi_\theta\,\|\,\pi_{\text{ref-KL}})\tag{3}\]

A.3 Training Algorithm

Algorithm 1 AC-RL (structure)
Require: D, V_θ, R, α, group size M, gradient steps K
Initialize π_θ ← V_θ; frozen clarifier π_ref; KL reference π_ref ← π_θ
for t = 1..T:
  sample batch B ~ D
  for each (I_j, Q_j) in B:
    for i = 1..M:
      c0 ~ π_θ(·|I_j,Q_j)
      q1 ~ R_clarify(·|Q_j,c0)              (no gradients)
      if q1 ≠ ∅: c1 ~ π_ref(...); A ~ R(·|Q_j,c0,(q1,c1)); C=1
      else: A ~ R(·|Q_j,c0); C=0
      R^(i,j) = 1.0 if correct ∧ C=0; α if correct ∧ C=1; 0 otherwise
    fit Beta params to {R^(i,j)}; compute BNPO advantages
  for k = 1..K: θ ← θ − η∇θ L_clip(θ; π_ref-KL)
return π_θ

B Unbiasedness of the Three-Tier Reward

[Summary] Theorem 1 states that if the post-action randomness \(\xi\) satisfies \(p_\theta(\tilde\tau)=p_\theta(\tau)\,p(\xi\mid\tau)\) with \(p(\xi\mid\tau)\) independent of \(\theta\), then the REINFORCE estimator with the tiered reward and an action-independent baseline is unbiased.

\[p_\theta(\tilde\tau)=p_\theta(\tau)\cdot p(\xi\mid\tau)\tag{4}\] \[R_{\text{tier}}(\tilde\tau)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if correct}(A(\tilde\tau))\wedge C(\tilde\tau)=0\\\alpha&\text{if correct}(A(\tilde\tau))\wedge C(\tilde\tau)>0\\0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\tag{5}\] \[J(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tilde\tau\sim p_\theta}[R_{\text{tier}}(\tilde\tau)]\tag{6}\] \[\hat g(\tilde\tau)=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\cdot(R_{\text{tier}}(\tilde\tau)-b_t(s_t))\tag{7}\]

The proof proceeds in three steps.

Step 1: Setup. The extended trajectory density factorizes as

\[p_\theta(\tilde\tau)=p(s_0)\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)P(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t)\cdot p(\xi\mid\tau)\tag{8}\]

Step 2: Policy gradient.

\[\nabla_\theta J(\theta)=\nabla_\theta\int p_\theta(\tau)p(\xi\mid\tau)R_{\text{tier}}(\tilde\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau\tag{9}\] \[=\int p_\theta(\tau)p(\xi\mid\tau)R_{\text{tier}}(\tilde\tau)\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(\tau)\,d\xi\,d\tau\tag{10}\] \[=\int p_\theta(\tilde\tau)R_{\text{tier}}(\tilde\tau)\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(\tau)\,d\tilde\tau\tag{11}\] \[\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(\tau)=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\tag{12}\] \[\nabla_\theta J(\theta)=\mathbb{E}_{\tilde\tau\sim p_\theta}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\cdot R_{\text{tier}}(\tilde\tau)\right]\tag{13}\]

Step 3: Baseline subtraction. The baseline term has zero expectation, so the estimator remains unbiased:

\[\mathbb{E}_{\tilde\tau\sim p_\theta}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)\cdot b_t(s_t)\right]\tag{14}\] \[=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}_{s_t}\left[b_t(s_t)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{a_t\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid s_t)}[\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)]\right]=0\tag{15}\] \[\mathbb{E}_{\tilde\tau\sim p_\theta}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t\mid s_t)(R_{\text{tier}}(\tilde\tau)-b_t(s_t))\right]=\nabla_\theta J(\theta)\tag{16}\]

[Summary] The paper then adds three further results:

  • Remark 2 and Proposition 3: if the reasoner depends on \(\theta\), the estimator must include the extra score term \(\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(\xi\mid\tau)\).
  • Corollary 4: PPO, GRPO and BNPO remain applicable with this reward, although they optimize surrogate objectives.

C Generation Diversity During Training

[Summary] AC-RL keeps a lower fraction of zero-variance (uniform-reward) batches than binary-reward RL, about 0.31 versus 0.42 at convergence.

D Prompt Templates

D.1 Vision-Language Model Prompts

[Summary] There are two captioner prompts:

  • vlm_initial_description_prompt: asks for a comprehensive description relevant to the question, without answering it. It covers objects, spatial relations, exact text, and visual attributes.
  • vlm_focused_description_prompt: given the original question, the previous description and a focus request, the model describes only the requested visual details, without solving or calculating.

D.2 Reasoner Prompts

[Summary] There are two reasoner prompts:

  • reasoner_adaptive_decision_prompt: the reasoner decides between SOLVED and NEED_MORE_INFO. It is instructed to be conservative and to make at most one specific request. The output fields are Reasoning, Status, Answer (boxed) and Request.
  • reasoner_final_prompt: the reasoner solves step by step, using <think> and <answer> tags and \boxed{} answers.
